## Source-linked observations

- 观察一：神童式早熟没有拖垮后来的成长。作者（Peacock）在回顾 Young 十四岁前即通希伯来、阿拉米、古叙利亚、撒玛利亚等东方语言时评论：多数早慧儿童"植株的活力"会被过早激发所耗尽，而 Young 的求知欲与意志不降反升；并认为他出身低微、所属教派戒律严格，反而救了他一命——免于被当作"神童"四处展览。原文："In the subsequent history of Young's education, we shall discover no symptoms of decay, either in the desire or power of acquiring knowledge, and the firmness of purpose with which he persevered in mastering the most difficult and repulsive studies, seems to have advanced with his increase of years. It was, perhaps, a fortunate circumstance for him that the modest station in life of his parents and connexions, and the severe habits of the sect to which they belonged, saved him in some degree at least from the misfortune of being paraded as a prodigy."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察二：同伴 Hodgkin 对十四岁 Young 的评价——"稳定与专注"近乎让一切学习目标都触手可及。此语出自曾与 Young 同窗的 John Hodgkin 写给 Peacock 的更正声明，是同时代人对 Young 早期心智的第一手观察。原文："deriving some advice and assistance in them, from the extraordinary youth, whose stability of conduct and intensity of application seemed to place every desirable object of literary or scientific pursuit within the reach of his astonishing mental powers."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察三：里士满公爵（Duke of Richmond）1794 年书信里对青年 Young 的印象：博学而不自命不凡、随和却毫无架子，连对自己的贵格会（Quaker）衣着与教派都谈得极为得体。这是同时代社交圈对 Young 社交举止的直接证词。原文："I really never saw a young man more pleasing and engaging. He seems to have already acquired much knowledge in most branches, and to be studious of obtaining more: it comes out without affectation on all subjects he talks upon. He is very cheerful and easy without assuming anything; and even on the peculiarity of his dress and Quakerism he talked so reasonably, that one cannot wish him to alter himself in any one particular."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察四：友人写给爱丁堡的引荐信把 Young 概括为"学者与哲学家的统一体"——当代技艺的教养与古代举止的朴素集于一身。此语被 Young 转引给叔父 Brocklesby 医生，作为同时代人对他的定型画像。原文："He unites the scholar with the philosopher, and the cultivation of modern arts with the simplicity of ancient manners."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察五：剑桥同窗的回忆指出他"从不炫耀学问"，被问到最难的问题时却以"轻快、果断"的口吻像谈家常一样作答；但同一位证人直言他"比我所认识的人更不适于传授知识"，并称剑桥青年半讥半敬地叫他"Phaenomenon Young"。这条第三方证词同时刻画了他的谦抑与交流短板。原文："He never obtruded his various learning in conversation; but if appealed to on the most difficult subject, he answered in a quick, flippant, decisive way, as if he was speaking of the most easy; and in this mode of talking he differed from all the clever men that I ever saw."以及"He was, therefore, worse calculated than any man I ever knew for the communication of knowledge."以及"He rarely associated with the young men of the college, who called him, with a mixture of derision and respect, 'Phaenomenon Young'; but he lived on familiar terms with the Fellows in the Common-room."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察六：同一位剑桥证人描述他的仪态仍带贵格会式的僵硬，从不说、不做无礼之事，却也不用任何客套；"自然无怯懦，从容无鲁莽"，从不主动给意见，也不以做实验为常。这是对 Young 性格内向、克制、不自我推销一面的直接刻画。原文："In his manners he had something of the stiffness of the Quaker remaining; and though he never said or did a rude thing, he never made use of any of the forms of politeness. Not that he avoided them through affectation; his behaviour was natural without timidity, and easy without boldness."以及"He seldom gave an opinion, and never volunteered one."以及"he was not in the habit of making experiments."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察七：Peacock 归纳 Young 的智力风格：他不信有什么特别的天赋禀异，认为人脑原始差异远小于一般设想；他"从不感到需要、也从不看重别人头脑所依赖的那种形式化证明过程"，却能凭"直觉把论证中相距遥远的点连接起来"而不借助普通心智所需的中间框架。这是对 Young 科学方法与论证风格（跳过常规形式推演、直接直觉综合）的最凝练概括。原文："He had little faith in any peculiar gifts of genius, believing the original difference between human intellects to be much less considerable than it was generally supposed to be."以及"he never felt the necessity nor appreciated the value of those formal processes of proof which other minds require."以及"extraordinary intuitive capacity of connecting distant points of a demonstration without the aid of the intermediate framework, whether of geometry or analysis, which ordinary minds require."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察八：作为医生的外部评价——病历显示他的处方"近乎当代最佳实践"，若再多活几年，其行医声誉"几乎肯定"会与他的哲学与学术声誉同样被承认；但 Peacock 同时指出他的仪态不利于行医："温文有礼，却不亲切"，从不表达超出所感的热情，也从不使用医生自我推荐的种种（哪怕完全正当的）手段。这是理解 Young 医学观与职业性格的关键一条。原文："the prescriptions which were given by Dr. Young are such as approximate — as nearly as circumstances will enable us to judge — to the best practice of the present day; and that if he had survived and practised his profession for a few years longer, his claims to distinction as a physician would have been, in all probability, as fully recognised as were those which were conceded to him as a philosopher and a scholar."以及"Dr. Young's manners were not favourable to his success as a practitioner: they were gentle and gentlemanly, but not genial; he never professed or expressed more than he felt, and resorted to none of those many, and we may add perfectly justifiable, arts by which some physicians recommend themselves to their patients."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察九：长期匿名发表、抗拒以真名示人，是他自我定位的一条鲜明线索。Peacock 记述：他"长期在与他专业无关的著作里保持匿名"，动机"不易解释"；成名后，密友们力劝他站出来，用他自己的话说就是"扔掉手杖和假发，把自己光秃秃的额头毫无防备、不戴伪装地亮给公众"。原文："Was he to persevere, as hitherto, in a vain attempt to preserve his anonymous character, now that his scientific celebrity was too great to be concealed, or was he, as he himself expressed it, 'to throw away his cane and wig and show his bare forehead to the public, undefended and without disguise?' His more intimate friends were strenuous in urging him to adopt this second course."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）

- 观察十：临终场景与身后定评——病榻上"心境全然镇静从容"，继续用铅笔编埃及语词典，说"若能活下来完成它固然欣慰，但即便不能，也为自己一生没有虚度过一天而感到满足"；死因为主动脉骨化，被归因于"从襁褓时代起就未曾间断的心智劳顿"。身后，皇家学会主席 Davies Gilbert 悼词称他"带着一种植根于众人期待的自信来到世上，而这一期待在他一生中被超额兑现"；Peacock 的总评是"他不折不扣地是一个求真之人"（对最平常的谈话也容不下丝毫夸大或修饰），"在人生的各种关系里，正直、仁慈、无可指摘，对同行研究者的帮助始终如一、毫无保留"。韦斯敏斯特教堂墓志铭刻着"几乎在人类知识的每一个部门都同样杰出……以不辍的劳作、直觉感知的能力，首先建立了光的波动说，首先穿透了遮蔽埃及象形文字千年的幽暗"。原文："nothing could be more striking than the entire calmness and composure of his mind, or could surpass the kindness of his affections to all around him."以及"it would still be a great satisfaction to him never to have spent an idle day in his life."以及"not brought on probably by the natural course of time, nor even by constitutional formation, but by unwearied and incessant labour of the mind from the earliest days of infancy."以及"came into the world with a confidence in his own talents growing out of an expectation of excellence entertained in common by all his friends, which expectation was more than realized in the progress of his future life."以及"Dr. Young was emphatically a man of truth."以及"Dr. Young was a man in all the relations of life, upright, kind-hearted, blameless. His domestic virtues were as exemplary as his talents were great. He was entirely free from either envy or jealousy, and the assistance which he gave to others engaged in the same lines of research with himself, was constant and unbounded."（1855，src-e90e236b56cc）
