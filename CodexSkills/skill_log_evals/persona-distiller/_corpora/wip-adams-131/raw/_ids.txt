# ==================== WHAT THIS CORPUS IS, IN ONE LINE ====================
# 83 signed verbatim remarks he made from the floor of A.I.E.E. meetings between 1907 and
# 1921, plus two whole first-person addresses, plus six signed papers, plus his own
# directory entry. The remarks are the point; the papers are nearly voiceless (see below).
#
# ==================== ★★★ THE SPEAKER LABEL, AND WHY SEARCHES MISS IT ====================
# The printed label is MIXED CASE and the initials sit on the line ABOVE the surname
# because of the column wrap:
#         ... Transformers have been built up to almost a million
#         volts.
#
#         Comfort A.
#         Adams: First, I want tosay that I fear Mr.
#         _ Peek has misinterpreted my statement, ...
# So:
#   * searching for ALL-CAPS "C. A. ADAMS:" / "COMFORT A. ADAMS:" returns ZERO speech --
#     ALL-CAPS "ADAMS:" in these volumes is a running head, never a speaker.
#   * searching for "MR. ADAMS:" / "PRESIDENT ADAMS:" returns ZERO -- the A.I.E.E.
#     Transactions never print an honorific before a discussant's name.
#   * anchoring the regex to the start of a line returns ZERO -- the label is mid-line.
#   * a window of N characters after the NAME may miss the first person, because the
#     remark can open with a question ("What kind of service was that?").
# The forms that actually occur, counted over the 31 volumes swept:
#     "C. A. Adams" 145 | "Comfort A. Adams" 70 | "C.A. Adams" | "C, A. Adams"
#     "Cs A. Adams" | "Cy Ar Adams" | "CLA, Adams" | "Comrort A. ADAMS" | "C. A. Adams, Jr."
# and two parentheticals: "Comfort A. Adams (by letter):" and
# "C. A. Adams (communicated after adjournment):".
#
# ==================== ★★★ THE THREE GUARDRAILS, AND WHAT EACH ONE CAUGHT ====================
# (1) THE SIGNATURE MUST TOLERATE "Jr.".  In Vol. XXIII (1904) he is printed
#     "C. A. Adams, Jr." because his FATHER bore the identical name Comfort Avery Adams.
#     Rejecting "Jr." would have lost src-0063 entirely -- a 14-page signed contribution.
#     Requiring "Jr." would have lost the other 82 remarks. Both spellings are admitted.
# (2) ALL-CAPS "ADAMS:" + A PAPER TITLE IS A RUNNING HEAD -- 210 of them were discarded
#     across the 31 volumes, and NOT ONE was speech. Per volume:
#        Vol. XXXII pt1 1913  72   ("ADAMS: RADIOACTIVITY")  -- and see the namesake note
#        Vol. XX     1903     48   ("ADAMS: HEYLAND MACHINE.")
#        Vol. XXIV   1905     34   ("ADAMS: DESIGN OF INDUCTION MOTORS.")
#        Vol. XXVIII 1909     20   ("ADAMS: WAVE-SHAPE IN ALTERNATORS")
#        Vol. XXXVIII pt1 1919 20  ("ADAMS: STANDARDIZATION")
#        Vol. XXIII  1904     14   ("ADAMS: REPULSION MOTOR.")
#        Vol. XXXIX pt1 1920   1 | Vol. XXXV pt1 1916  1  (officer lists, not heads)
#     In Vols. XX and XXIV the real number of remarks is 0 and 0, so without this
#     guardrail those two volumes alone would have manufactured 82 phantom "remarks".
# (3) PROCEEDINGS / TRANSACTIONS DOUBLE PAGINATION -- deduplicated by title+year, never by
#     journal+pages. A.I.E.E. printed the same paper twice with two page ranges. Two rows
#     were prevented from becoming four: "The Design of Induction Motors" (NAS memoir cites
#     24:327-62; the Transactions printing starts at p. 649) and "Pole-Face Losses" (NAS
#     cites 28:1063-86; a 1916 footnote in Vol. XXXV cites Vol. XXVIII Pt II p. 1151).
#     Separately, the slice of "Electromotive Force Wave-Shape in Alternators" STOPS where
#     its own discussion begins, so his remarks in that discussion are counted once, in
#     src-0005, and not a second time inside the paper.
#
# ==================== ★★ NAMESAKES: EIGHT OF THEM, ONE IS HIS FATHER ====================
#   Comfort Avery Adams (the father)  Warren, Ohio postmaster/editor, later a Cleveland
#        wholesale clothing merchant. Identical name. Not an engineer, so he does not
#        appear in these journals -- but he is why the 1904 volume prints "Jr.".
#   Conrad A. Adams        abbreviates to "C. A. Adams". Who's Who in Engineering 1925,
#        the entry immediately after his. Keene, N.H., vocational-education teacher.
#   Edwin Plimpton Adams   ★ NOT IN THE PROBE'S LIST -- found here. Author of the five
#        lectures "RADIOACTIVITY" (Trans. Vol. XXXII, 1913), byline "BY EDWIN PLIMPTON
#        ADAMS". All 72 "ADAMS: RADIOACTIVITY" running heads in that volume are HIS.
#   Lee F. Adams           ★ NOT IN THE PROBE'S LIST -- found here. Wrote the BEARINGS
#        section of the 1915 motor symposium (Vol. XXXIV pt1). Discarded.
#   Alton D. Adams / Edward D. Adams   middle initial D. Not him. Edward D. Adams appears
#        as a donor in library reports; the semicolon in "gift of Mr. Edward D. Adams;"
#        reads as a speaker colon and was discarded.
#   P. H. Adams / H. H. Adams          discussants in the same volumes, same label format.
#        "P. H. Adams: We are using a maximum setting of two seconds..." (Vol. XXXVIII pt1)
#        is indistinguishable from his rows except by the initials.
# RULE APPLIED: "Jr." -> him. Middle initial other than A. -> not him. Bare "C. A. Adams"
# -> admitted only where the subject is A.I.E.E. machinery, insulation, testing, rating,
# standardization or society governance, which is what every admitted row is about.
# NOTHING was admitted on the surname alone.
#

# ==================== ★ THREE DATES DELIBERATELY LEFT BLANK ====================
# (a) THE LAMME MEDAL YEAR IS UNRESOLVED AND IS NOT WRITTEN HERE. Two sources disagree.
#     The NAS memoir names one year and simultaneously calls the award "the thirteenth
#     presentation of this medal"; the medal was first given in 1928, so counting forward
#     puts the thirteenth presentation four years earlier than the year the memoir names,
#     and a contemporaneous Electrical Engineering citation agrees with the COUNT, not with
#     the memoir's year. The IEEE's official Lamme recipient list could not be retrieved on
#     this pass (the guessed URL returns HTML, not the PDF). Neither candidate year is
#     recorded anywhere in this corpus. UNRESOLVED.
# (b) HIS DEATH DATE IS NOT WRITTEN HERE. It is carried only by secondary works: a 1965 NAS
#     memoir, itself still inside the copyright term, and modern reference works. No
#     contemporaneous obituary was opened, because an obituary published in the year he
#     died is inside the copyright term and outside pre1929. UNRESOLVED on this route.
#     Nothing in this corpus should be read as fixing it.
# WHAT THE CORPUS DOES FIX: he was speaking from the floor of A.I.E.E. meetings from 1907

# Research Council's Engineering Division, as printed in the 1922 Journal rosters.
# The 1956 Edison Medal IS documented, by the IEEE's own recipient list, and the NAS memoir
# omits it. Being CHAIRMAN of the Edison Medal Board of Awards is a different fact from
# RECEIVING the Edison Medal; both are true and neither cancels the other.
