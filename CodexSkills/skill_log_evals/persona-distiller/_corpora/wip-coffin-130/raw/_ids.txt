# ==================== THE NAMESAKE RULE APPLIED IN THIS CORPUS ====================
# There are two Coffins in the 1885-1926 American electrical record and they overlap
# heavily in period, in trade press, and in subject-adjacent vocabulary:
#
#   WANTED   Charles L. Coffin -- Detroit, Michigan. Welding processes, electrodes,
#            joints, patents. Later assigns to the ELECTRIC METAL WORKING COMPANY.
#   NOT      Charles A. Coffin -- Lynn/Boston, Massachusetts. President of Thomson-
#            Houston Electric Co., then first president of General Electric. Company
#            affairs, mergers, boards, addresses. Born 1844-12-31, died 1926-07-14.
#
# EVERY row below was admitted only on evidence printed ON THE PAGE that ties the
# document to Detroit and/or to welding/metal-working -- never on the bare surname.
# Material about the other man, and material where the two could not be told apart,
# was NOT taken; those decisions are logged at the bottom of this file under
# "NOT TAKEN -- NAMESAKE".
#
# ==================== BIRTH AND DEATH YEARS: STILL UNPROVEN ====================
# The English Wikipedia article "Charles L. Coffin" gives 1844-1926. Those are the
# same four digits as Charles A. Coffin's, and that article never mentions General
# Electric. It is treated here as a suspected conflation. NO birth or death year is
# asserted anywhere in this corpus. Nothing below should be read as confirming them.
# What the primary record DOES fix is his residence over time, and that is recorded.

THE ONLY NON-PRIMARY ROW HERE, and the only contemporaneous evidence of how his work was RECEIVED. A research library's chronological bibliography of the world literature on electric welding to 1913. It records a named "Coffin process" / "Coffin arc process" being described in English, French and German periodicals from 1890 to 1911 alongside Benardos, Slavianoff, Thomson, Zerener, Lagrange-Hoho and Ries; and it records that the Coffin patent was publicly CONTESTED in 1890 (annotation under A. NAMESAKE CHECK: all 17 occurrences attach the name to a welding process, never to company affairs; Thomson and "Thomson-Houston" appear in the same bibliography as separate competing processes, which is itself evidence the two Coffins are different men.
